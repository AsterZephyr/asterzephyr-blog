A meeting recording: the screen shows a quarterly budget sheet, the host says "let's go with version two," and someone next to them cuts in, "wait, version two hasn't been approved yet." If the task is to turn the budget sheet into a spreadsheet, all the evidence is in the picture. If the task is to determine which version the meeting finally approved, that interruption, who spoke first, and which page the screen was on while they spoke can all change the answer. The two tasks live in the same video file, yet they need different evidence.

After reading the Qwen3.8-Omni technical report, a question comes up naturally: Qwen3.8-Max can already understand images, documents, and video, and it can operate interfaces and write code. Why also have an Omni?

The two sides do not disclose the same depth: the [official Qwen3.8-Max release post](https://qwen.ai/blog?id=qwen3.8) mainly covers workflows, training system ideas, and evaluations, while the [Qwen3.8-Omni technical report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25611) gives details on perception encoding, pretraining, and the real-time system. The official API documentation is taken as of October 1, 2026.

## Two Models, Three Entry Points

In the current official vision model documentation, `qwen3.8-max` takes text, images, and video as input and outputs text. The same page states explicitly that when you need to understand the sound in a video or feed audio directly, you should switch to `qwen3.8-omni-flash`, which also outputs text. Real-time voice and video sessions correspond to a third entry point, `qwen3.8-omni-flash-realtime`. [Source: vision model documentation](https://docs.qwencloud.com/developer-guides/getting-started/vision-models), [speech-to-speech model documentation](https://docs.qwencloud.com/developer-guides/speech/s2s-models)

| Public endpoint | Key input boundary | Output and interaction mode | What to watch when comparing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Qwen3.8-Max | Text, images, video | Text; can call tools | The documentation does not list raw audio input |
| Qwen3.8-Omni-Flash | Text plus audio-video understanding, including sound | Text | The name "Omni" does not mean this endpoint outputs speech |
| Qwen3.8-Omni-Flash-Realtime | Real-time audio, images, video, etc. | Real-time text and voice sessions | The report lists it as non-thinking, so the reasoning settings of ordinary Flash do not carry over |

<TechGraph src="/images/posts/qwen38-max-与-omni-同一段录像各看到了什么/g01-endpoints.svg" title="ONE FAMILY, DIFFERENT ENDPOINTS" alt="Input and output comparison of the three public endpoints: Max, Omni-Flash, and Omni-Flash-Realtime" caption="Diagram: look at the model family and the specific endpoints separately (drawn by the author of this post, based on the official vision-models and s2s-models documentation, checked on 2026-10-01)" />

This table answers a question about public product boundaries. Max not listing audio input does not prove its internal architecture can never add audio, nor does it say whether some Qwen product wires up speech recognition in the background. What it does answer is an engineering question: if raw sound is evidence the task requires, you cannot treat Max's support for `video` as support for sound.

The origins of the two models also need to be kept apart. The Max release post says it is based on Qwen3.5, scaled to 2.4T total parameters and 95B active parameters; the Omni report says its language backbone inherits the sparse MoE line of Qwen3.8-Next. Nothing in the public material says "add an audio head to Max and you get Omni," nor that Omni's teacher is Max. They belong to the same family and share some technical directions, but there is no evidence of direct inheritance, distillation, or weight relationships. [Source: introduction of the official Max release post](https://qwen.ai/blog?id=qwen3.8), Omni report Section 2, PDF page 3

So rather than ranking "is Max or Omni stronger," it is more useful to ask about three dimensions separately: general task ability, the range of input evidence, and runtime interaction constraints. A name can hint at positioning, but it cannot replace these three checks.

## How Many Kinds of Evidence Are in One Recording

A video container can hold picture, audio track, subtitles, and timing information all at once, but what the model finally receives depends on the endpoint, the media processing, and how the input is organized. Frames only, frames plus subtitles, and frames jointly input with the raw audio track preserved are three different observation conditions.

The picture can tell the model the column names and amounts in the budget sheet, the area being pointed at, and which page the screen share switched to. Subtitles can tell it who said which words. Raw sound also carries pauses, overlapping speech, emphasis in tone, alarms, and background events; if the recording device kept suitable spatial channels, where a sound came from is yet another kind of information.

<TechGraph src="/images/posts/qwen38-max-与-omni-同一段录像各看到了什么/g02-evidence.svg" title="WHAT SURVIVES PREPROCESSING?" alt="Four kinds of evidence in a meeting video: picture, transcribed text, raw sound, and spatiotemporal relationships" caption="Diagram: the same video contains different evidence, and preprocessing decides which information enters the model (drawn by the author of this post, based on Omni report Section 2; the meeting is a hypothetical scenario for explanation)" />

Transcribing speech into text first and then handing it to Max is a perfectly reasonable approach. If the task is only to extract dates and amounts spoken aloud, a transcript may be enough. The cost is that the downstream model can only judge based on what the upstream step kept: a transcript with no speaker separation, no timestamps, and no overlap markers flattens relationships such as "who interrupted whom" and "which shared slide this sentence corresponds to." A longer context cannot make up for this loss. Context can hold more of what was preserved, but if a sound was discarded before input, the model has no way of knowing an alarm went off there. Likewise, copying a mono recording into multiple channels cannot recover a sound source direction that was never recorded.

Conversely, keeping the audio track is not a sufficient condition either. Whether the streams sit on the same timeline, whether frame sampling missed a key action, and whether the transcript misheard a negation all need checking. A native multimodal model moves part of the fusion work into the model, but capture and input organization still decide what it can see and hear.

## As Qwen3.8-Max Sees It: Video Is Picture, Subtitles, and Work Feedback

### How Video Gets into Max

The Max release post describes long video quite concretely: faced with hundreds of hours of video, it can locate segments, answer detailed questions, and organize people, events, time, and scenes into a video Graph memory, building connections across long spans. [Source: "Multimodal Agent" section of the official Max release post](https://qwen.ai/blog?id=qwen3.8)

The evaluation settings make this path even clearer. In the multimodal results table of the Max release post, VideoMME and VideoMME v2 are marked **w/ Sub.**, meaning they were evaluated with subtitles enabled, and Max scores 90.4 and 68.3 respectively; LVBench and EgoLife have a separate **w/ Mem.** column, evaluated with a memory system built on Qwen-MM-Plugins, where Max's LVBench goes from 81.8 to 85.6; VideoDR is marked **w/ Search**, using a search tool.

These annotations show that in Max's public evaluations, the language information in a video mainly enters through the text channel of subtitles, and long time spans are supported by a memory system on the Harness side. This is an evaluation configuration, not a claim about the upper limit of Max's ability. But put back into that meeting recording, what Max can reliably get is the picture, the subtitle text, and the event structure organized by the memory tool; the tone of the interruption and the overlap of two people speaking at once depend on whether the upstream transcript kept them.

"Hundreds of hours of video organized into retrievable memory" and "hundreds of hours of raw video stuffed into one API request" are not the same thing: the current official documentation limits video input for both Max and Omni-Flash to at most 2 hours and within 2GB. [Source: "Video support" in the vision model documentation](https://docs.qwencloud.com/developer-guides/getting-started/vision-models)

### Vision Is Not Only on the Input Side

The more important side of Max's multimodality is that it connects vision to action. The release post stresses that during execution the model keeps observing its own intermediate artifacts, checking page layout, object orientation, spatial relationships, animation effects, and interaction results, and after finding problems such as a TV placed upside down or a misaligned interface, it replans and corrects itself. Vision thus changes from "understanding the input" into a feedback loop running through planning, execution, verification, and iteration.

Take "recreating an app from a screenshot of its interface." The first version of the artifact may only resemble it in color and text. After opening the page, the model still has to judge whether a button is covered, whether the layout breaks when the window shrinks, and whether the state really changes after a click. Code builds the interface, and the running interface in turn checks the code. The official name for this route of combining programming with GUI operation is Hybrid Agent: programming completes large amounts of work efficiently, and GUI operation brings back feedback from the real running system.

<TechGraph src="/images/posts/qwen38-max-与-omni-同一段录像各看到了什么/g03-visual-loop.svg" title="VISION INSIDE THE WORK LOOP" alt="A goal passes through code or GUI execution to produce an actual result; Max observes the result and corrects it, and a verifier checks completion" caption="Diagram: vision enters the planning, execution, and correction loop (drawn by the author of this post, based on the 'Multimodal Agent' section of the official Max release post; the verifier in the figure is a generalization of the public mechanism)" />

RecreationBench is one concrete task definition for this route: the model faces a black-box app with no source code and no network access, can only understand its behavior through interaction, and then recreates it from scratch, on platforms covering Ubuntu, macOS, Windows, Android, and Web. In the release post's table, Max scores 51.7 on this internal benchmark. It tests the repeated loop of observing, implementing, and looking back, which needs completely different evidence from judging who first raised an objection in a multi-person meeting.

Model output and system artifacts also need to be distinguished here. Max's endpoint output is text: it generates code, tool parameters, or operation instructions, and then tools render pages, build Blender scenes, edit video, or perform GUI operations. Being able to deliver these artifacts end to end shows that the model and toolchain work well together; it does not justify rewriting Max's output type as "native images, video, and 3D scenes."

### The Training System Has to Make Feedback Work Reliably

The "Office Assistant" section of the Max release post discloses three interrelated training system designs. They are three parallel pillars, not sequential training stages.

**Environments scale by combining dimensions.** Task ranges from single tasks to multiple tasks and tasks spanning days; Workspace ranges from a few files to hierarchical directories and heterogeneous materials; Harness comes in different categories, versions, and skills. The three can be combined, and environment coverage grows with the combinations, so there is no need to build a complete environment from scratch for each new situation. This is very practical for agent training: for the same "revise the report," the execution trajectory is completely different depending on whether the materials are in one file, scattered across several directories, or need to be confirmed with a browser; a tool with the same name may also return feedback in different formats in different Harnesses. The model has to learn to move the work forward according to the current environment, rather than memorizing a fixed sequence of tools.

**A unified reward system handles heterogeneous verification.** The signals listed officially include execution-based checks, rubric evaluation of text and of rendered visual artifacts, and agentic checks. Whether a script runs, whether a document is compliant, and whether a web page is visually complete cannot be judged by the same string match. Unified means one system organizes these different checking methods: recreating a page requires looking at the rendered result, data processing requires checking the calculations, and the criteria are not merged into one. The full verifier implementation and weights have not been disclosed.

**An online data balancer reorganizes each batch.** It keeps the distribution within each batch balanced by task, difficulty, workspace, and Harness; the authors explain this as reducing gradient variance across batches so that the training compute for reinforcement learning can keep scaling stably. If a run of similar easy tasks gets sampled and is then suddenly replaced by a run of complex long-horizon tasks, the distribution of the training signal swings sharply. Controlling the mix ratio is exactly one of the links that turns "many environments" into "stable training."

This is how Max's working ability is organized by the training system. The full RL algorithm, reward weights, environment scale, and training cost have not been disclosed, and there is no perception encoder configuration that could be compared against Omni's. [Source: "Office Assistant" section of the official Max release post](https://qwen.ai/blog?id=qwen3.8)

## As Qwen3.8-Omni Sees It: Sound and the Moment It Happens

Switching to Omni's point of view, what is added in the same recording is that audio track. The architecture in the Omni report: a vision encoder, the AuT audio encoder, and the Spatial AuT spatial audio encoder each connect through an adapter to a shared Thinker, and text also enters the same reasoning backbone. The first problem to solve is representation alignment, turning different inputs into a sequence the backbone can use together, rather than each writing its own summary first and then stitching them together. [Source: Omni report Section 2, PDF pp. 3-4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25611)

### AuT Keeps More Than a Transcript

The ordinary AuT path processes audio as 16kHz sampling and 128-dimensional Mel features, with a 25ms window and 10ms stride; then four Conv2D modules perform 16x downsampling in total, producing a feature sequence of about 6.25Hz. Figure 2 also draws 32 layers of self-attention, and finally an adapter connects to the language backbone.

<Figure src="/images/posts/qwen38-max-与-omni-同一段录像各看到了什么/p02-audio-encoders.png" alt="Figure 2 of the original Omni report: audio encoding paths of ordinary AuT and Spatial AuT" caption="Source: Figure 2 of the original Qwen3.8-Omni report, PDF page 4; faithful crop only" />

These numbers show that sound is compressed before it enters shared reasoning: 10ms is the initial time resolution, and about 160ms per position is the interval of the encoder output; the two are not at the same layer. A rough calculation at 6.25Hz gives about 375 feature positions at that layer per minute of sound; this number excludes video, text, spatial channels, and other sequence overhead, and it is not the endpoint's token billing formula. Still less is 6.25Hz a playback sampling rate or a response speed: how long after speaking a user hears a reply also depends on session commit, model computation, speech generation, the network, and the playback buffer.

AuT is trained with ASR supervision, but at runtime what is handed to the Thinker is a learned audio representation, not "text the sound was forced into." The ASR objective helps the representation learn linguistic information, while the joint perception path aims to let the shared model use the sound itself, as well as the relationship between sound and picture. How many nonverbal cues are actually kept ultimately has to be judged by task results.

### Why Spatial Sound Takes a Separate Path

Spatial AuT's input is FOA (First-Order Ambisonics). The report stresses keeping both the real and imaginary parts of the complex STFT, that is, preserving both magnitude and phase information. Ordinary Mel features are suited to expressing the content of a sound, but they cannot replace cross-channel spatial relationships. A separate spatial path gives the model a chance to use additional evidence such as sound source direction.

Back in the meeting room, spatial information might help tell who is speaking, provided the recording conditions are in place: whether the device captured FOA, whether the channel conventions are correct, and whether the sound field is mixed. Having a spatial encoding path in the model cannot recover data the microphone never recorded; and the report describing this architecture does not mean every cloud audio endpoint exposes full FOA input.

### Multiple Inputs Share One Timeline

Just putting sound tokens and image tokens into the same context does not guarantee the model knows they happened at the same time. In a meeting, "version two" might be said before the screen share switches pages, or it might be rejected after the switch; the same word appearing at different moments can carry completely different meaning for the task. The Omni report keeps explicit text timestamps; spatial tokens in the same time slice share a temporal ID and are distinguished by different spatial IDs, so temporal and spatial relationships have a place to be expressed.

This also suggests something for system design: events extracted by the model should ideally point back to a time range in the original media, so evidence can be retrieved again later. Explicit temporal information is an architectural fact from the report; a traceable event object is an engineering suggestion drawn from it, not something the report gives. Whether the timestamps are precise enough and whether clocks on different devices agree still has to be tested on real input.

### Learn to Connect First, Then Learn to Work Together

Omni's four-stage pretraining unfolds along this difficulty. S1 freezes the language backbone, training the adapters first and then the encoders, so each perception path connects to the existing backbone; S2 unfreezes all parameters for joint training, so the backbone adapts to shared multimodal input. S3 keeps dense attention and freezes the backbone, letting only the QSA indexer learn from the supervision given by full attention; only S4 enables QSA, training the backbone and indexer jointly. Connect first and then change together; teach the indexer to pick content first and then let the whole model adapt to the computation path after selection. This is the order the report discloses, and the report does not give item-by-item ablations. All four pretraining stages run at length 262,144, and the final 1M context is extended only after post-training. [Source: report Section 3, PDF pp. 5-6](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25611)

Post-training faces a different problem: audio, vision, text, tools, and long-task abilities have to coexist in the same student. The report's approach is to first train expert teachers in different directions, each going through SFT and RL, then merge their trajectories into a single student, and finally run unified RL; rewards for long tasks look at actual execution results, not at the model's own claim of completion. [Source: report Section 4, PDF page 6](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25611)

<TechGraph src="/images/posts/qwen38-max-与-omni-同一段录像各看到了什么/g04-training.svg" title="TWO VIEWS OF AGENT TRAINING" alt="Max's organization of environments and rewards, and Omni's perception integration, sparse training, and merging of teacher trajectories" caption="Diagram: the training emphases disclosed in the two public materials (drawn by the author of this post, based on the 'Office Assistant' section of the official Max release post and Omni report Sections 3-4; does not imply that the two use the same training recipe)" />

Looking at the training on both sides together, the difference lies in what each chose to disclose: Max discloses how it organizes environments, feedback, and batches so that working ability can scale stably; Omni discloses how it connects multiple perception paths into one backbone and then merges multiple abilities into one student. What the figure does not draw does not mean the model lacks it: Max also needs input representations, and Omni also needs working environments and result verification, and its post-training and experiments include tools, programming, and long-horizon tasks. So do not simplify the two into "Omni handles listening and seeing, Max handles thinking."

Omni's side also has gaps: the report does not disclose the Thinker's full RL algorithm and reward mix, and the GSPO used in the Talker's final stage cannot be used to fill that gap; the initialization of the vision encoder is written as Next in some paragraphs and Qwen3.5 in others, so the specific checkpoint cannot be determined.

## When the Conversation Becomes Real-Time

Suppose that meeting is not a recording but collaboration happening live. The user says "prepare it per version two," the system has already started answering, and the user adds, "don't send it yet, change it to version three." The problem is no longer just understanding a sentence: whether to stop the old answer, whether to update the task parameters, and how to handle background work that has already started all follow from it.

An ordinary text endpoint can use `stream=True` to return text chunk by chunk. A real-time voice system has to keep receiving input, commit the current segment, maintain session state, and play sound while generating it; the official Realtime documentation describes this mechanism with WebSocket / WebRTC sessions and the text and audio output configuration in `session.update`. Both are called "streaming," but their runtime semantics differ. [Source: Realtime multimodal speech guide](https://docs.qwencloud.com/developer-guides/speech/realtime-multimodal-speech)

The Thinker-Talker path in the Omni report has a clear division of labor: the Thinker handles understanding and text content; the Talker generates acoustic codes with RVQ and MTP, where MTP predicts the remaining RVQ codebooks **within the same audio frame**, not future frames of audio; causal Code2Wav turns acoustic codes into audio, with output extended to 48kHz; ARIA lets speech keep generating based on a partial text prefix, without waiting for the whole answer to be written. [Source: report Section 7, PDF pp. 14-15](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25611)

<Figure src="/images/posts/qwen38-max-与-omni-同一段录像各看到了什么/p06-realtime.png" alt="Figure 3 of the original Omni report: runtime structure for real-time speech, tools, and Live Harness" caption="Source: Figure 3 of the original Qwen3.8-Omni report, PDF page 15; faithful crop only" />

This path explains why this family can generate speech, while you still have to pick the right endpoint when calling it: the audio-video understanding entry of ordinary Omni-Flash outputs text, and only the Realtime entry corresponds to real-time voice sessions, which is a non-thinking version, so the long thinking budget of ordinary reasoning models cannot be copied over.

Latency also needs to be read in pieces. The first-text and first-audio timings in report Table 9 start from commit; input upload happens before timing starts, VAD was turned off in testing, and connection setup and the playback-side buffer are not included. It measures generation performance under specified conditions and cannot represent the total time "from the user starting to speak to the background delivering a file." A real system has at least three kinds of waiting: the response to what is being said right now, the time to gather enough evidence, and the time for background execution to finish. Counting tens of seconds of file export into the first speech packet confuses execution with interaction; treating an instant confirmation as task completion makes the opposite mistake.

Qwen-Live-Harness offers a useful structure: the foreground delegates a task and confirms immediately, the background keeps executing and monitoring, and feedback is organized after completion, so real-time interaction and long tasks do not have to squeeze into the same blocking path. [Source: report Section 7.3, PDF page 16](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25611)

## Connecting the Two Views in One Task

Suppose you want to build a meeting assistant that has to understand the live discussion and also organize materials, edit files, and generate pages. One workable division of labor: observation that needs raw sound and real-time sessions go to Omni, and the later work suited to Max or another execution model goes to them. This is **an engineering design suggestion**; there is no officially prescribed fixed pipeline of Omni feeding Max, and it has not been tested against the API.

<TechGraph src="/images/posts/qwen38-max-与-omni-同一段录像各看到了什么/g05-handoff.svg" title="PROPOSED EVIDENCE-PRESERVING HANDOFF" alt="Engineering proposal: the real-time foreground produces evidence objects with pointers to the original media, hands them to an optional execution backend, and gives feedback after tool results are verified" caption="Diagram: foreground-background collaboration that preserves raw evidence (drawn by the author of this post; an engineering proposal inspired by Omni report Section 7 and Max's visual workflow, not an official fixed model combination)" />

### The Handoff Object Must Lead Back to Raw Evidence

If the foreground hands over only one sentence, "the meeting decided on version three," the background will treat a perceptual judgment as an established fact. Later, if you want to check who proposed the change, whether there were objections, and which file version three corresponds to, it is hard to locate any of it again.

A more robust handoff object keeps the event identifier, time of occurrence, observed content, location in the original media, and questions still to be confirmed. The example structure is a suggested format, not an official API schema; `unresolved` holds items the system recorded as needing verification, not a probability calibrated by the model.

```json
{
  "event_id": "meeting-042-revision-03",
  "time_range_ms": [124000, 130500],
  "observation": "The host proposed switching to version three and asked to hold off on sending",
  "evidence": [
    { "media_id": "meeting-042", "kind": "audio", "start_ms": 124000, "end_ms": 130500 },
    { "media_id": "meeting-042", "kind": "frame", "time_ms": 127000 }
  ],
  "proposed_action": "Prepare version three materials and wait for the send instruction",
  "unresolved": ["The file corresponding to version three has not been verified"]
}
```

The background does not have to accept the foreground's conclusion wholesale; what it receives is an entry point for going back to check: find the version three file, compare version differences, and when necessary retrieve the corresponding audio and frames again. Only when the original media pointers, the summary, and the proposed action are kept together do observation and execution connect. The same goes for long video: Max's video Graph memory in its release post and the question-driven step-by-step segment retrieval in the Omni report both deal with "how historical material stays usable." The Max release post also cites Qwen-MM-Plugins, which is a Harness extension library, not a model structure unique to Omni. [Source: "Multimodal Agent" section of the Max release post](https://qwen.ai/blog?id=qwen3.8), Omni report Section 5.1, PDF page 7

### Real-Time Changes Need Versions and Task State

When the user changes version two to version three, the perception side should produce a new event, and the execution side has to know which version it is preparing. You can keep a `task_id`, an input revision number, and the execution state in the background: once an old event is superseded by a higher revision number, plans not yet executed are updated accordingly, and materials already generated are rechecked.

Stopping foreground speech and cancelling background tasks also need to be separated. A user interrupting an explanation may only want to add information; when a task is explicitly cancelled, the background has to confirm that the cancellation really took effect. "Speech interrupted," "parameters revised," and "task cancelled" should be three different state changes, not all lumped into one stop. Resent audio events, network reconnects, and monitoring polls can all deliver the same intent to the background multiple times; event identifiers and task state let the system tell whether this is a duplicate notification, a task update, or a new task. The model is responsible for understanding intent, while persistent state and idempotent execution are handled by the Harness and the tool layer.

When the background declares completion, it should hand over real artifacts and verification records: whether the spreadsheet opens, whether the page actually renders, and whether the send succeeded are three different checks. The foreground replies to the user based on these, rather than wrapping up on the model's last line, "it's done."

### Many Tasks Do Not Need Two Models

Recognizing a table in a screenshot, adjusting a web page layout, or processing plain text materials that have already been reliably transcribed: starting from a vision and text workflow is enough. When raw sound does not affect the result, adding an audio path only adds one more component to manage. When you must distinguish off-screen sound, overlapping speech, and sound-picture relationships, the input has to keep this evidence, and only then does Omni's raw sound path have clear value; if low-wait voice interaction is also required, then add a Realtime session and delegate the slow execution. Omni can do agent tasks itself, so there is no need to bolt on a Max just to make the architecture diagram look good.

These are routing principles based on input boundaries, not a measured cost-effectiveness ranking. Whether to choose a single model, ASR plus an execution model, native Omni, or a foreground-background combination has to be decided by comparing accuracy, waiting time, call cost, and failure recovery on your own tasks.

## How Far the Public Results Can Prove Things

Both materials have plenty of evaluations, but they do not add up to a condition-matched Max versus Omni comparison. Many video tasks in the Max release post use subtitles, memory, or search, and the Harness and statistical conventions differ by benchmark; Omni's text tables mainly compare against Qwen3.8-Flash, not Max, and swapping the Flash column for Max would change what the paper means. Omni report Table 7 also includes Video-MME-v2 (static 65.0, Qwen Code 71.3), but its input mode differs from the subtitle-enabled 68.3 in the Max table, so ranking them together is meaningless.

More convincing is a comparison inside the Omni report: how the results change for the same Omni when it understands long video from static input versus when it actively gathers evidence through Qwen Code.

<Figure src="/images/posts/qwen38-max-与-omni-同一段录像各看到了什么/p03-token-evidence.png" alt="Table 8 of the original Omni report: accuracy and tokens per query for the same model under static input and Qwen Code active understanding settings" caption="Source: Table 8 of the original Qwen3.8-Omni report, PDF page 14; faithful crop only. The comparison is between two ways of using the same Omni model" />

In Table 8, OmniVideoBench accuracy rises from 63.4 to 67.8, an increase of 4.4 percentage points; tokens per query drop from 145,736 to 79,117, a reduction of about 45.7%, with cross-turn context retained in the count. Under this model and task setting, organizing observation first and then gathering evidence on demand improved both answer quality and input consumption. It does not measure Max as better or worse than Omni, nor does it show that total latency or the bill will drop proportionally: multi-turn tool calls carry overhead for media processing, requests, and execution, and billing may also differ by input type.

Omni did not improve on every sound task either. In Table 4, compared with the previous-generation model in the table, WenetNet's WER goes from 3.7 to 4.8 and FLEURS from 7.2 to 9.3, where lower WER is better. Wider capability coverage does not guarantee every kind of transcription task gets stronger; if the need is only ASR, test directly with your own languages, noise, and speaking conditions. [Source: Omni report Table 4, PDF page 11](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25611)

Max's version also needs to be kept separate from the one at release. The official announcement states that since September 5, 2026, `qwen3.8-max` has switched to the `qwen3.8-max-0902` snapshot, so the model evaluated in the August 3 release post should not be assumed to be exactly the same as the version you get by calling the alias today. [Source: Max update announcement](https://docs.qwencloud.com/changelog/model-updates/qwen3.8-max-upgrade)

To really compare the two, you have to fix at least the raw media, how sound and subtitles are preserved, tool permissions, the Harness, the number of retries, the model version, and the timing start point; meeting tasks also need a check that key decisions can be traced back to the original segment, and desktop tasks need verification of real application state. Comparing only whether the final answers read smoothly misses the relationship between evidence and action.

## Back to That Recording

From Qwen3.8-Max's point of view, this recording is picture, subtitles, and events that can be organized into memory. It is good at turning what it sees into work, and at repeatedly looking back at results and correcting itself during execution; its training system organizes this ability around environment combination, unified rewards, and batch balancing. From Qwen3.8-Omni's point of view, the recording also holds raw sound, the direction of the sound, and the moment it happens. These signals go through their own encoders into the same reasoning backbone and then, through real-time sessions, become immediate responses in conversation.

So the answer to "Max already exists, so why Omni" depends on which piece of evidence the task is missing: the budget sheet needs reliable visual and document understanding, who rejected which version needs sound and temporal relationships, and putting changes into files needs tool execution and verification. The two models overlap in ability, their public endpoints have clear boundaries, and a system can pick one of them or let them work together.

Before building anything, ask three questions: Does the input keep the raw evidence the task needs? Can conclusions drawn from perception point back to their original location? Is "done" backed by real tool results?
