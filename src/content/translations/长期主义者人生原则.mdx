## I. Introduction

### What Are Principles?

Principles are ways of thinking about the problems you encounter in work and life. They distill past experience into something you can use repeatedly, helping you respond more quickly to whatever comes next.

- In technical terms, they are the `lib` you have built up—a library that helps you reuse your own experience and the experience of others.

### Why Write These Principles Down and Make Them Public?

1. **Think more deliberately**: rather than solving each problem in isolation, work through its layers and preserve the underlying causes and reasoning.
2. **Keep refining my understanding**: principles reflect what I currently understand and change as I grow. Writing them down gives me a foundation to build on and helps keep my thinking from remaining superficial.
3. **Collaborate better**: making my thinking open to others helps us understand one another and communicate more efficiently. We can spend our energy on actual disagreements instead of repeatedly establishing things we already agree on.
4. **Hold one another accountable**: knowing is easy; doing is hard. If my actions contradict my words, please point it out. That can open a deeper discussion about whether the problem lies in the principle or in how I put it into practice.
5. **Develop these ideas through discussion**: I hope we can discuss and refine them together, gradually taking the thinking further.
6. **Leave an intellectual legacy**: if I am fortunate enough to keep this up, these principles will be a valuable legacy to leave to future generations.

### How to Read These Principles

- **The order matters**: the numbered lists below express priorities. Without an order, they would become little more than a collection of platitudes.
- **They can change**: the present order reflects my current understanding and may change as that understanding develops.

## II. General Principles: The Core Logic of Decisions

1. **Life and health come first**: without your life, every possibility disappears. By the same reasoning, efficiency and time must always be part of the calculation.
2. **Maintain a margin of safety—protect the downside**:
    - Anything important needs more than one line of defense. Have at least two.
    - Technical reliability cannot rest on a single fallback. Have at least two, whether at the system level or the process level.
    - Keep looking for weak points in the system and repair them promptly, rather than waiting to be caught off guard.
    - Everyone makes mistakes, so always double-check.
3. **Minimize regret**: follow your heart. When choosing, first ask whether, a few years from now, you would regret not doing this.
4. **Do your part and accept what lies beyond your control**: let goals guide you, but focus on the process rather than the outcome.
5. **Keep decisions reversible whenever possible**: every technical upgrade should have a corresponding rollback plan.
6. **Pursue excellence within limits**: for things that are not important, meeting the basic requirements is enough; save your energy. Strive for excellence at work and cultivate contentment in life.
7. **Choose the right moment to decide**: make the decision when you have 50%–70% of the information you need. Do not wait for 100%—that is too slow—but do not decide with too little, either; that is too hasty.
8. **Apply the 80/20 rule**.
9. **Stay open and humble**: knowledge is boundless, while our lives are finite. Ignorance is not humanity's greatest enemy; arrogance is.
10. **The difficult choice is often the right one**: do not take a shortcut in the hope that you will simply get lucky.
11. **Learn from other people's mistakes**: draw on others' time and experience to raise your own ceiling.

## III. Dealing with People and Working with Others

1. **Be sincere and candid**: no pretense and no talking around the point.
2. **Do not do to others what you would not want done to you**.
3. **All people are equal**: do not look down on others.
4. **Start with kindness**: repay kindness with kindness, blood with blood, and never forget a debt of gratitude.
5. **Honor commitments**: once you make a promise, do your best to keep it. If you cannot, let people know in advance.
6. **Mutual benefit is the foundation of collaboration**: without the prospect of a win for both sides, there is no point discussing a partnership. Reciprocity can extend over the long term.
7. **Rank information by credibility**: opinion < consensus < statistics < experiments < double-blind experiments (A/B-Testing) < established natural-science knowledge grounded in physical principles.

## IV. Spending and Investing

1. **Put time first**: paying money to save time is usually worthwhile. You can earn more money; your total supply of time is fixed.
2. **Prioritize compounding**: not only money, but knowledge, experiences, friendships, and social relationships can compound.
3. **Life is a succession of experiences**: do not simply wear away the hours. Use spending to improve the experience of living.
4. **Believe that you get what you pay for**:
    - In a sufficiently competitive market, do not spend too much energy hunting for something both excellent and cheap. Put that energy into creating value.
    - What seems expensive often includes a premium for reputation and brand, which can be worthwhile when buying long-term services. But do not expect too much from the outcome; accept the imperfections that occur with ordinary probability.

## V. Managing a Team

### General Principles

1. **Lead by example**.
2. **Pay attention to the team's emotions**: emotions are not a matter of right or wrong; help people work through them promptly. Unresolved negative feelings may resurface much more forcefully one day.

### Selecting People

1. **Character comes first**: look for candor and integrity, not clever maneuvering. A failure here is a veto.
2. **Potential vs. ability**: if I must choose between potential and sheer current ability, I will most likely choose potential.

### Designing the Organization

1. **First principle: high cohesion, low coupling**: this is a prerequisite for effective collaboration.
2. **Do not insist on a one-to-one match with business teams**: especially when the business is still in flux, I prefer to give things some time to play out.

### Developing People

1. **Your growth is your responsibility**: you are the main actor; your leader is a resource. Do not wait passively, depend on others, or expect things to be handed to you.
2. **Training and self-motivation play different roles**: employee training can raise the floor. Raising the ceiling requires self-motivation.

### Planning and Assigning Work

1. **Find a win for both the business and people's development**: every arrangement of work has an optimal solution—a global optimum. If you have not found it, keep looking.
2. **What a leader should expect**: doing the work to the best of one's ability is enough. A leader's energy should go into setting direction and guiding the process. Outcomes have many causes; do not become captive to a specific target number.

## VI. Technical Principles

1. **Simplicity comes first**: keep everything simple; simple things are less likely to go wrong. Ideally, a program should do only one thing.
    - Do not expect to design one complex system that solves every problem. However abundant your resources, start by solving a few specific problems.
2. **Prioritize reliability, then extensibility**: reliability supports survival; extensibility supports growth.
3. **Design for the likely cases**: solve 99% of the problems and set black swans aside. Once the design is complete, add separate contingency plans for black swans, keeping them decoupled.
4. **Make full use of other people's code**: at the same time, make your own code as easy as possible for others to use.

## VII. Business Principles

1. **The user's perspective comes first**: begin by thinking about the problem from the user's point of view.
2. **Deliver a prototype as early as possible**: even if it is imperfect, putting it in users' hands sooner lets you iterate faster.

## VIII. Family and Life

1. **Family**: harmony at home matters far more than deciding who is right and who is wrong.
2. **Stay clear-eyed about life**:
    - **Life is worth living**: only you can experience your own life, so your life can never be a failure.
    - **Be optimistic in the long term and pessimistic in the short term**: the future will get better, but probably not tomorrow or next month.
